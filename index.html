Modeling How Literacy Develops

How do we know what we know about how literacy develops? This week looks at three methodological lenses researchers use to answer that question: growth curve modeling, item response theory, and connectionist/agent-based models.

Facilitator: Ashley Ippolito Format: Asynchronous Est. time: 45–60 min
Objectives What To Do Video Materials Deep Dives Discussion Activity
1

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

  • Describe how growth curve modeling, item response theory, and connectionist/agent-based models are each used to study literacy development.
  • Compare the assumptions each method makes about how literacy skills emerge.
  • Identify which of these methodological lenses might apply to your own research question.

Seasonal Perturbations in Longitudinal Growth: Piecewise Modeling of Summer Reading Loss

How to methodologically capture non-linear, discontinuous academic growth over time: piecewise latent growth curve modeling, differential seasonal effects across learner profiles, and model fit selection (RMSEA, SRMR, log-likelihood) for comparing linear, quadratic, and piecewise growth models.

Psychometrics of Early Literacy: IRT Item Banking & NAEP Scale Linkage

The psychometric architecture behind measuring reading ability across grade levels: 1PL, 2PL, and 3PL logistic models (difficulty, discrimination, pseudo-guessing), conditional 2D-IRT linkage of ECLS-K trajectory classes to the NAEP Grade 4 scale, and construct validity/item misfit in large-scale item banks.

Coming Soon

Computational Architectures of Word Recognition: DRC vs. Connectionist PDP Models

Theoretical models of word-level reading and what they imply for cognitive psychology and instruction: the rule-based Dual-Route Cascaded (DRC) model, Seidenberg's Parallel Distributed Processing "triangle" model linking orthography, phonology, and semantics, and how statistical learning and working memory shape decoding practice.

Watch

Executive Function as a Developmental Covariate in Early Reading

The structural relationships between working memory, cognitive flexibility, and reading trajectories: direct assessment measures (Numbers Reversed, Dimensional Change Card Sort), teacher-reported behavioral constructs, and executive function as predictor, co-occurring trait, or mediator of early literacy difficulty.

Discussion Prompts

1. Pick one of the three modeling approaches and describe how you might apply it, or a method it inspires, to a question in your own research. What would it let you see that your current approach can't?

3. These methods are often treated as more "objective" than qualitative approaches. Push back on or defend that idea: what does a model like this actually decide for us, and what's still left to human judgment?

Activity: Choosing the Right Lens

Scenario A

A district wants to track individual students' oral reading fluency growth across a school year to identify which intervention groups show the steepest gains.

Scenario B

A team is developing a new phonological awareness screening tool and needs to determine which items best distinguish between students who do and don't have reading difficulties.

Scenario C

A researcher wants to understand why children with dyslexia show a specific error pattern when reading irregular words.
